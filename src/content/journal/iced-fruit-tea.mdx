Iced tea made from loose fruit tea is better than anything from a bottle, and it takes about ten minutes of actual work. There are two ways to do it, and they suit different teas.

## Hot-brewed, poured over ice

This is the fast way. Brew the tea at **double strength**: twice the usual amount of tea in the same amount of hot water, steeped for the usual time. Strain it straight over a tall glass packed with ice. The ice melts down to the right strength as the tea cools.

It works best with hibiscus blends like <ProductMention slug="cran-pom" />, which need hot water to bring out their deep ruby color and tart fruit.

## Cold-brewed overnight

This is the patient way, and the smoother one. Put about one and a half times the usual amount of tea in a jar of cold water, cover it, and leave it in the fridge for six to eight hours. Strain and drink within two days.

Cold water pulls out less bitterness, so cold brew tastes rounder and naturally sweeter. It's the best way to make <ProductMention slug="blueberry-lemonade" /> and <ProductMention slug="shade" />.

<Callout title="The color trick">
  Blueberry Lemonade brews a deep blue from butterfly pea flower. Squeeze in lemon and it turns violet in the glass. Do it in front of someone.
</Callout>

## A few small things

- Iced tea tastes less sweet than hot. Taste before you add anything, then add a little at a time.
- Freeze leftover tea in an ice cube tray, so your next glass doesn't get watered down.
- A sprig of mint, a few frozen berries, or a thin slice of citrus all go a long way.
