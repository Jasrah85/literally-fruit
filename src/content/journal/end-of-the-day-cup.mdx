There's a cup of tea that isn't about the tea at all. It's the one you make after the dishes are done, when the house finally goes quiet and nobody needs anything from you for a while.

That cup should be caffeine-free, a little warm-spiced or floral, and slow to make. Here's how we make ours.

## Pick a caffeine-free blend

Most of our Artisan Infusions were made for this time of day:

- <ProductMention slug="unwind" />: tulsi, rose, and orange peel. Warm, gently spiced, a little sweet on its own.
- <ProductMention slug="hearth" />: rooibos, pear, and vanilla. Round and dessert-like, and very good with warm milk.
- <ProductMention slug="lights-out" />: linden and mint. Honeyed, floral, and the last cup of the night.

## Make it slowly

Use water just off the boil, and cover the cup while it steeps so the aroma stays in. Herbal blends take longer than tea, five to ten minutes, so this is the moment to stop doing other things. Wash one dish, or don't.

<Callout>
  Rooibos never turns bitter, so Hearth can sit in the cup as long as you like. Linden gets fuller the longer it steeps; Lights Out is best between seven and ten minutes.
</Callout>

## Make it yours

A spoonful of honey suits almost every herbal blend. Warm milk turns Hearth into a latte. And if you'd rather have something fruitier, <ProductMention slug="cran-pom" /> is caffeine-free too.
